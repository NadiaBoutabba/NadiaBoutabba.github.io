About

I am a physicist working in quantum optics and optoelectronics. My research looks at how light interacts with matter: exact solutions of the Bloch equations, control of the refractive index through laser pulse shape, the Kerr effect, slow light and solitons.

I have taught physics at undergraduate level for more than twenty years in Tunisia, Canada, the United States and the United Arab Emirates. At Texas A&M University I taught electromagnetism to 240 students alongside Nobel laureate David Lee. I also have administrative experience: institutional accreditation, research committees and research ethics, and running a physics learning-success centre at Cégep Saint-Laurent in Montréal.

I review papers for Optics Express, New Journal of Physics, JOSA B and several other journals, and hold IOP Trusted Reviewer status.

Research /

Pulse shaping and the Bloch equations

How the shape of a laser pulse controls what an atom does, and how to solve the equations exactly.

Overview

Short laser pulses with shaped envelopes and chirped frequencies drive the populations of atomic levels. My work derives exact analytical solutions of the Bloch equations for two-level atoms, with dephasing, for hyperbolic, double-exponential-quotient and q-deformed pulses, and applies them to multi-level atoms such as rubidium 87. A recent paper extends these solutions to nuclear magnetic resonance signals.

Selected work

q-deformed laser pulses and population inversion

N. Boutabba, S. Grira, H. Eleuch, Scientific Reports 12 (1), 9308, 2022

An exact solution of the Bloch equations for a two-level atom driven by a q-deformed hyperbolic pulse, followed by a study of a three-level rubidium-87 atom. Starting from a non-inverted population, a stationary inversion of about 20% is reached at resonance, which matters for quantum information processing and quantum memory. The asymmetry parameter q alone is enough to steer the dynamics of the system.

Negative refractive index and left-handed media

Media where light bends the “wrong” way, and how to tune them.

Overview

In left-handed media the refractive index is negative, which opens applications such as super-resolution imaging, cloaking and sub-wavelength imaging. I study atomic systems that reach a negative index through the coupling of electric and magnetic responses, and how control fields, pulse shape and thermal reservoirs tune it.

Selected work

Dichroism and birefringence in a left-handed atomic medium

H. Waleed Abbasi, Y. Saeed, N. Boutabba, H. Ali, Physical Review A 112 (4), 043506, 2025

A new way to obtain birefringence and dichroism in a negative refractive index system lit by left and right circularly polarised light. The system is optically active: it rotates the polarisation plane by −6° with weak absorption, and the dichroism peaks grow fourfold when the control field goes from 1γ to 2.5γ. These peaks are of interest for designing optical filters.

Tunable refractive index in a left-handed medium

N. Boutabba, Journal of Optics 23 (7), 075504, 2021

In an optically dense four-level atomic system, the shape of the control pulse (double Gaussian, hyperbolic tangent, exponential quotient or exponential power) modulates the refractive index spectrum. An index that can be tuned at will is directly useful for photonic devices.

Kerr effect, light drag and the Casimir force

Nonlinear effects in dense atomic media.

Overview

Dense atomic media show strong nonlinear effects. I study the Kerr effect under magneto-electric coupling, light drag (lateral and rotary) in left-handed and gain-assisted media, and the quantum control of the Casimir force through induced chirality and spontaneously generated coherence.

Selected work

Kerr effect in a three-level system under magneto-electric coupling

N. Boutabba, Journal of Optics 20 (2), 025102, 2018

Analysis of the Kerr effect in a negative index material coupled to a thermal reservoir. Refractive and gain spectra come from solving the density-matrix equations up to third order, with a local-field correction. The Kerr nonlinearity is large and comes from the electromagnetic components of the fields.

Research /

Organic optoelectronics and photovoltaics

Modelling charge transport and photocurrent in organic devices.

Overview

My doctoral work studied organic semiconductor devices: photovoltaic cells (Al/6T/ITO), light-emitting diodes (ITO/AVB/Al) and quantum-well laser diodes. Their nonlinear behaviour is analysed with non-commutative Volterra series, which gives analytical results to compare with experiments.

Selected work

Organic photovoltaic cells and Volterra series

N. Boutabba et al., Synthetic Metals 139 (2), 2003 · Organic Electronics 4 (1), 2003 · Synthetic Metals 128 (3), 2002

Analytical solution of the nonlinear rate equations of an Al/6T/ITO cell using non-commutative Volterra series, in transient and steady state. The second order is not negligible, contrary to common assumption, and the calculated spectra match measurements. The results suggest an experimental method to estimate carrier lifetimes and the Langevin recombination rate.
